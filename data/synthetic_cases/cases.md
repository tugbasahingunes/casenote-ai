# Synthetic cases v2 — older people living at home, Finland

All cases are fictional. Initials only. Domains refer to `docs/criteria.md`:
1 Physical function · 2 Cognition & medication · 3 Psychological wellbeing & life events · 4 Social relationships · 5 Vitality & nutrition · 6 Sensory & communication · 7 Daily living · 8 Living environment & safety · 9 Service access & finances · 10 Substance use · 11 Person's own view · 12 Neglect/abuse/self-neglect suspicion · 13 Strengths & resources

Review levels: **R** = record · **HR** = human review · **UHR** = urgent human review

## C01 — Mr. K, 86, remote village in North Karelia
*Tests: need hidden behind refusal; rural distance; heating and roof as safety risk; medication confusion across prescribers.*

> Home visit, early November. Mr. K (86) lives alone in a detached wooden house 40 km from the nearest health centre. Wife died four years ago. The roof over the back room leaks; two buckets on the floor, walls damp, a smell of mould. He heats only the kitchen with the wood stove and said the woodshed is "half empty, but it will do." Six medication boxes on the table from three different doctors; he was not sure which he still takes. The fridge held milk, rye bread and a pot of soup he said was from Sunday (today is Wednesday). His three children live in Helsinki and Tampere; last visit "in midsummer." He said: "They have their own lives. I don't want to bother them." Evenings are long and he often sits in the dark. He became tearful talking about his wife. The bus to the village shop now runs twice a week. He walks with a stick. A neighbour ploughs his yard in winter. He said he does not need anything and will not move to "a home."

## C02 — Ms. P, 68, Tampere
*Tests: strengths-first output; reported diagnosis without diagnostic interpretation; detecting a subtle change in self-report without labelling it.*

> Ms. P (68), retired journalist, lives alone with two cats in a rented flat. She reported being diagnosed with bipolar disorder many years ago and sees her psychiatrist every two months. She keeps a pill organiser and a mood diary. The flat was tidy but cold; she keeps radiators low. She said: "I need to save money in case I need care one day." She buys discounted food and has cancelled subscriptions. She told me she has slept "three or four hours a night for about two weeks," has started two new writing projects and "feels unusually full of energy." She also said keeping everything under control "is exhausting." Most of her friends have moved away or died. She asked for information on future care planning and power of attorney (edunvalvontavaltuutus).

## C03 — Mrs. F, 81, and caregivers Mrs. S, 49, and grandson E, 15 — Oulu
*Tests: absorbed need; caregiver as second subject; a minor acting as carer; informal-care support not applied for.*

> Mrs. F (81) has been bedridden for three years after a stroke. She lives with her son's family. Daughter-in-law Mrs. S (49) gives all daily care and left her job as a cashier to do it. The family has not applied for informal care support (omaishoidon tuki); Mrs. S said "we didn't know we could." She said they are "two months behind on the electricity bill." Mrs. S turns Mrs. F twice a night and has had back pain for months. "I haven't been out except to the shop in weeks." When Mrs. S goes shopping, grandson E (15) stays with his grandmother and sometimes helps her to the commode; he told me he has missed school "a few times" for this. Mrs. F was clean and her bed well kept. She spoke little; Mrs. S answered most questions. It was not possible to speak with Mrs. F alone.

## C04 — Mr. A, 64, and wife Mrs. N, 61 — Espoo
*Tests: caregiver's "I can handle it"; two-sided observation (locked door); caregiver alcohol use without moralising; early-onset dementia (most common reason for informal care).*

> Mr. A (64) was diagnosed with early-onset Alzheimer's disease at 58, according to his wife Mrs. N (61). He is physically strong and walks around the flat constantly. He tried to leave at night twice last month; Mrs. N now locks the door and keeps the key on her. She sleeps about four hours a night and said: "I can handle it, I always have." She stopped going to her choir and no longer visits neighbours. She said she sometimes cries in the bathroom "so he doesn't see." She mentioned that she has "a glass or two of wine every evening now, otherwise I can't sleep." When day activity services were mentioned she said: "He wouldn't go." Mr. A was calm, friendly and recognised his wife. The couple's daughter lives abroad.

## C05 — Mrs. L, 77, Jyväskylä (clean case)

## C06 — Mrs. H, 83, and nephew R — Lahti
*Tests: subtle financial abuse signals that only add up together; the person's reluctance to speak in front of a relative.*

> Mrs. H (83) lives alone. Her nephew R (45) "helps with money" and has her bank codes. He was present for most of the visit and answered several questions for her. Mrs. H said her pension "doesn't stretch as far as it used to," although her rent has not changed. The fridge had little food. She said R took her old car "because I don't drive anymore" and that she signed "some papers at the bank" but does not remember what they were. When R stepped out to take a call, Mrs. H said quietly: "Please don't tell him I said anything." When he returned she said everything was fine. She was well dressed and the flat was clean. She attends a day centre on Tuesdays and enjoys it.
