# Assessment criteria — older people living at home (Finland)

CaseNote AI scans a home-visit note across **13 domains**. Each domain is grounded in the Finnish Act on Services for Older Persons, one of two WHO frameworks, or a documentation gap identified in Finnish practice research.

The purpose is not to decide anything. It is to make visible the signals a professional might miss, together with the sentence in the note that supports them.

The criteria come from three layers:

- **Legal:** the provisions of the Act on Services for Older Persons that define what an assessment must consider, and the duty to notify.
- **Academic:** the WHO International Classification of Functioning (ICF) and the WHO Integrated Care for Older People framework (ICOPE).
- **Practice:** Finnish research showing which areas service-need assessments tend to under-document.

*RAI is a licensed interRAI instrument. This document does not use RAI items; the criteria are independently written.*

## Domains

The first four domains come directly from the Act. The others draw on the WHO frameworks, Finnish practice research and the duty to notify. The "signals" column gives examples, not an exhaustive list.

| # | Domain | Signals in the note (examples) | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Physical function and mobility | Falls, difficulty walking or with stairs, unable to get out of bed, help needed with washing or dressing, new pain | §15 physical; ICOPE locomotion; ICF mobility and self-care |
| 2 | Cognition and medication management | Forgetfulness, confusion about time or place, getting lost, forgetting or mixing up medication, medication list not reviewed for a long time, difficulty making decisions | §15 cognitive; ICOPE cognition; research on medication safety in family care |
| 3 | Psychological wellbeing and life events | Feeling low or hopeless, loss of interest, anxiety, grief; recent bereavement, move, or discharge from hospital | §15 psychological; ICOPE psychological domain; Eastern Uusimaa preventive-visit study |
| 4 | Social functioning and relationships | Living alone, reduced contact with others, relationship problems, exhausted family carer | §15 social; ICF interpersonal relationships; Alatalo et al. 2014 |
| 5 | Vitality and nutrition | Appetite loss, unintended weight loss, empty fridge, low fluid intake | ICOPE vitality |
| 6 | Sensory function and communication | Difficulty seeing or hearing, unable to use the phone, language barrier, breakdown in communication | ICOPE sensory capacity; ICF communication; Turku study showing this area is recorded less with age |
| 7 | Daily living and household tasks | Difficulty shopping, cooking, cleaning or managing money | ICF domestic life |
| 8 | Living environment and safety | Accessibility of the home, heating problems, fire or accident risk, leaving the home at night | §15 environment; ICF environmental factors; Turku study |
| 9 | Service access and financial conditions | Unaware of services, transport difficulties, digital exclusion, unpaid bills | §15 environment; ICF environmental factors |
| 10 | Substance use | Observation or statement about alcohol or medication use | Alatalo et al. 2014 (dependencies overlooked) |
| 11 | The person's own view and preferences | What the person wants and does not want; needs in their own words; whether their view could be obtained | §15 joint assessment; Puustinen et al. 2020 |
| 12 | Suspected neglect, abuse or self-neglect | Unable to look after oneself, unexplained injury, a relative controlling the person, money being taken | §25 duty to notify |
| 13 | Strengths and resources | What the person can do independently, supportive relatives, hobbies, neighbours, coping strategies | Turku study (recognising individual resources) |

## Flagging rules

Each flag is linked to a domain, a sentence in the note and a review level. People are never scored.

1. **Evidence quote required.** Every flag quotes the sentence from the note word for word. Nothing that is not in the note is flagged. No inference, no guessing.
2. **Three review levels:**
   - *Record:* the information belongs in the case record; no further action may be needed.
   - *Human review:* there may be a change or a need for support; a professional should assess it.
   - *Urgent human review:* signals in domain 12, or situations that immediately affect the person's safety. The system does not notify anyone; it directs the professional to consider their obligation under §25.
3. **Coverage gap report.** Domains with no information in the note are listed as "no information". This makes visible the social and psychological areas that assessments tend to miss.
4. **The person's voice is kept separate.** What the older person said and what the professional observed are marked separately ("person said" / "professional observed").
5. **No diagnostic language.** The system does not use diagnostic terms such as "dementia" or "depression" of its own accord, and only reports observed behaviour or statements. A diagnosis the person or a relative *reported* may be quoted as reported.
6. **Every output is a draft.** Nothing enters the case record until a professional approves it.

**Additional checks (based on Finnish documentation analyses):**

- **Need–action check:** if the note identifies a need but records no action or plan, it is marked "no action recorded". Inconsistencies between needs and interventions have been found in home care plans ([Puustinen et al.](https://www.utupub.fi/handle/11111/49548)).
- **Life event flag:** events such as losing a spouse or close relative are flagged separately; identifying these groups was found important in preventive home visits ([Eastern Uusimaa](https://www.theseus.fi/handle/10024/4598)).
- **Strengths balance:** the output lists resources from domain 13, not only problems; recognising individual resources is considered necessary for person-centred care ([Turku](https://www.utupub.fi/handle/11111/44256)).

**Rules derived from the synthetic case set:**

- **Said vs. observed:** when the person says "I don't need anything" or "I can handle it" but the observations in the note suggest otherwise, the system places the statement and the contradicting observations side by side and routes them to human review. The person's words are never discarded; they are shown together with the observations.
- **The caregiver as a second subject:** when the note describes a family carer, their situation is flagged in a separate section, apart from the cared-for person's domains. Areas: care burden and health, psychological wellbeing, social isolation, work and income loss, substance use, own view. This keeps the need absorbed by the carer from disappearing inside the cared-for person's "needs met" record.
- **Young carers:** when a child or adolescent carries care tasks, this is flagged as its own subject for human review.
- **Interpreted conversations:** when the person's view was obtained only through a family member acting as interpreter, domain 11 is flagged, because the view was not obtained independently.

## Scope and limits

This version is deliberately narrow.

- **No diagnosis or clinical assessment.** Health conditions are not interpreted.
- **No eligibility decisions.** The system does not decide which services a person should receive; that could fall under Annex III of the EU AI Act.
- **No RAI scores or items.** RAI is licensed and administered by trained professionals.
- **Synthetic data only.** No real case data is used.
- **Language:** the first version is in English. Finnish and Swedish documentation practices and national record standards are for a later version.
- **Target group:** older people living at home only. Residential care, families with children or disability cases would need separate criteria files.
- **Open question:** the criteria have not yet been validated by practising professionals in Finland. This is on the roadmap.
